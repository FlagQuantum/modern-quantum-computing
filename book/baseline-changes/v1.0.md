# Writing Baseline 1.0 decision record

Date: October 20, 2026. Baseline ID / Git tag: writing-baseline-v1.0.

The author requested closure of the full contents, prerequisite order, section depth and volume proposal into an executable writing baseline, with no further standalone paper auditing. This record adopts the existing scholarly architecture for production; it does not invent specialist approval, target-reader testing or completed chapter text.

The retained scope is nine subject parts, 50 global chapters, 435 stable sections, 1,045 direct prerequisite edges and 19 reader routes. The seven-volume allocation and the existing P/F/M/R/V obligations are adopted. No section, proof depth, direct dependency, theoretical topic or existing public section anchor is removed by this change.

The new production overlay comprises 16 batches and a complete 435-section queue. Dependency closure inserts section 10.7 before its H2-route consumer and sections 9.1, 9.2, 9.3 and 9.7 before core compiler consumers. This is a writing-order bridge, not a change to the underlying reader graph or a demand to finish the whole information volume first.

The immediate assignment is Chapter 1, sections 1.1–1.6; six authoring briefs are prepared. Three earlier starters (6, 23, 49) retain their existing draft/PDF status and do not count as accepted sections. No new chapter body is authored in this baseline change.

The previous all-series drafting pause is superseded. A section may enter drafting after its local brief, prerequisite explanation, exact source check and independent-reference design are ready. Mathematical, implementation or justified N/A, reader and rendering evidence remain conditions for publication acceptance. This resolves the circular requirement to obtain reader evidence from a complete unwritten series before any drafting begins.

Existing paper-audit records are parked as reference material. Open source limits attach to the affected optional claim; they do not schedule a continuing investigation or block the rest of the book. Required scientific facts are verified when the section needs them.

The scope digest covers chapter/section identity and order, exact prerequisites, core/advanced role, page envelopes, result/assumptions/depth/mandatory bridges, reader routes, volume allocation, appendices and batch targets. It excludes actual review/work status and in-scope citation/prose refinements. Git preserves the full baseline inputs. Later material amendments record old/new scope, affected IDs and downstream validation before updating the baseline version and digest.
