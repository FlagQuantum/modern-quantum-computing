docs/chapters/ch-computational-models-complexity-and-quantum-advantage.md:1906: The count C=∑zᵣ lies in {0,…,R} and fits in ⌈log₂(R+1)⌉ bits. The implementation uses `R.bit_length()`, which equals that ceiling for every positive integer R: if 2ᵃ≤R<2ᵃ⁺¹, then a<log₂(R+1)≤a+1. The rational report C/R can be encoded as two unsigned integers of that width, using at most twice as many payload bits; common framing or printing digits is additional. A straightforward streaming counter reads R bits and uses R additions on O(log R)-bit integers, giving an O(R log R) bit-operation upper bound and O(log R) counter space. Retaining the raw record tensor instead uses its full output storage. These are different postprocessing choices, not incompatible estimates.
