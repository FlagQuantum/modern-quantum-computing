**A.** For label a, sum full probabilities over strings b whose q3,q1 bits equal a in that order. The corresponding projector is diagonal on precisely those strings; it includes q0,q2 as unrestricted coordinates. `[0]` removes one execution dimension, not a qubit dimension. The separate author transfer executes 17 four-qubit cases, checking the prepared vectors with an independent indexed-gate reference and then the native marginal with compatible-index sums.

**B.** Orthogonality follows from the finite geometric sum $sum_{j=0}^3e^{2pi i j(k-ell)/4}=4delta_{kell}$. Thus $Q_0=sum_{k=0}^1|b_k
anglelangle b_k|$, $Q_1=sum_{k=2}^3|b_k
anglelangle b_k|$ are orthogonal complete rank-two projectors. Their probabilities are respectively $sum_{k=0}^1|langle b_k|psi
angle|^2$ and $sum_{k=2}^3|langle b_k|psi
angle|^2$. The separate author transfer uses three new prepared states, adding three circuits for twenty transfer circuits total. It computes classical predictions on simulated vectors and does not execute a general-projector hardware measurement. It is author validation, not independent review.

**C.** The missing P1 subspace makes the family incomplete; on input $|1\rangle$ its total would be zero. Completeness must hold on the full state space. A zero-probability event for a particular input is compatible with a complete family and retains its label.

**D.** Interpreting $langlepsi|P_a|psi
angle$ as an outcome probability is the physical postulate. Nonnegativity and unit total follow from Hermitian orthogonal complete projectors and normalization. A conditional state additionally needs an update rule, treated in 6.2. A probability query returns deterministic ideal model predictions; a shot would return one label according to an outcome distribution, with finite-sample reasoning developed in 6.6.
